Before you read

1. Break the word down

Look at the word dysmorphia on its own — no title yet. In Greek, dys- means "bad/abnormal" and morph means "form/shape". You may already know it from the term "body dysmorphia".

Before reading on, jot down a guess:

  • What field do you usually hear dysmorphia used in?
  • By analogy, what might money dysmorphia mean?

2. Think it over

Consider these questions for a moment before you read:

  • Is there a difference between how much money someone has and how rich they feel?
  • Have you ever spent money to avoid feeling embarrassed, even when you didn't need to?
  • Does social media change how people think about wealth?
Reading
Money Dysmorphia

Maria always assumed that anxiety about money would disappear the moment her bank balance grew. But now, earning more than she ever imagined, she still checks her banking app with a queasy stomach every morning, half expecting to find she's in overdraft. It makes no sense on paper — yet the fear won't lift.

This is what psychologists increasingly call money dysmorphia: a distorted image of reality in which how safe you feel with money has almost nothing to do with how much you actually have. Someone can be financially comfortable and still feel poor; someone else can have very little and feel oddly secure.

For some, like Maria, the response is to hoard money compulsively, refusing to spend even on essentials, terrified that a sense of scarcity is only ever one bill away. For others, it works the other way entirely: they go all out on things they can't really afford, buying rounds of drinks or upgrading their phone every year, because spending publicly feels like proof they're doing fine.

Neither pattern is really about arithmetic. It's about old beliefs — that money defines worth, that admitting to having shortcomings means failure — and about a culture that never stops asking how much you're worth, in every sense of the word. Perhaps it's worth pausing occasionally to contemplate your life goals and ask whether money is really the measure you want to use.
